# English Academic Writing Templates

## Introduction Templates

### Opening Hook Sentences
```
- "In today's [industry/environment], [phenomenon] has become increasingly [important/prevalent], with [statistic or trend] (Citation)."
- "[Phenomenon X] represents one of the most [critical/pressing/significant] challenges facing [organizations/consumers/managers] today (Citation)."
- "Despite [widespread belief / decades of research], [surprising gap or contradiction] remains poorly understood."
- "The rise of [trend/technology/phenomenon] has fundamentally transformed how [actors] [do what], raising important questions about [topic]."
```

### Literature Summary Sentences
```
- "A growing body of research has examined [topic], consistently demonstrating that [general finding] (Author1, Year; Author2, Year)."
- "Prior work has established [finding], with scholars highlighting the role of [factor] in shaping [outcome] (Citation)."
- "Existing research offers important insights into [X], yet most studies have focused on [narrow scope], leaving [broader context] largely unexamined."
- "Scholars in [field] have long recognized [phenomenon], and numerous studies have investigated [related constructs] (Author, Year)."
```

### Research Gap Sentences
```
- "However, little is known about [X] in the context of [Y]."
- "Despite this progress, we have limited understanding of whether/how/when [X affects Y]."
- "Yet, the mechanisms underlying [X→Y relationship] remain unclear."
- "Although prior research has examined [A], the role of [B] has received scant attention."
- "Existing studies have predominantly focused on [context A], leaving the question of [context B] underexplored."
- "The theoretical and empirical evidence regarding [X] is mixed, calling for further investigation."
- "To our knowledge, no study has simultaneously examined [X and Y]."
```

### Research Purpose Sentences
```
- "Drawing on [Theory], this study examines [X] in the context of [Y]."
- "To address this gap, we investigate how/whether/when [X] influences [Y]."
- "The present research aims to [purpose], thereby contributing to [stream of literature]."
- "We advance the literature by [contribution 1] and [contribution 2]."
```

### Contribution Sentences
```
- "This research makes several contributions. First, we extend [Theory] by [showing/demonstrating/arguing that]... Second, we contribute to the literature on [X] by..."
- "Our findings have important theoretical implications for [stream1] and [stream2], as well as practical implications for [managers/practitioners] who..."
- "By integrating [Theory A] with [Theory B], we provide a more nuanced understanding of..."
```

## Literature Review Templates

### Section Opening Sentences
```
- "[Construct X] refers to [definition] (Author, Year). Scholars have conceptualized [X] as [key dimension1] and [key dimension2]."
- "The concept of [X] was first introduced by [Author (Year)] to describe [original context]. Since then, research on [X] has expanded to include [related topics]."
- "Research on [X] has a long tradition in [field], dating back to [Author (Year)]'s seminal work on [topic]."
```

### Evidence Integration Sentences
```
- "Consistent with this view, [Author (Year)] found that [finding], while [Author2 (Year)] demonstrated [related finding] in the context of [different context]."
- "Building on [Author (Year)]'s framework, subsequent research has shown that [extension/refinement of finding]."
- "In contrast, [Author (Year)] argued that [counter-perspective], suggesting that [implication]."
- "[Finding] has been replicated across multiple contexts, including [context1] (Author, Year) and [context2] (Author, Year)."
```

### Critical Evaluation Sentences
```
- "However, [Author (Year)]'s study relied on [limitation, e.g., student sample], limiting its generalizability to [other populations]."
- "While these findings are informative, most existing work has focused on [narrow scope], and we know less about [broader question]."
- "The mixed findings in this literature may be attributed to [methodological differences / boundary conditions not yet identified]."
```

### Transition Sentences Between Sections
```
- "Having reviewed the literature on [X], we now turn to [Y], which serves as a key [mediator/moderator/outcome] in our model."
- "The preceding review establishes [X] as an important [antecedent/outcome]. We next examine [Y], which may help explain [the mechanism/when this effect occurs]."
- "Taken together, the above discussion suggests [summary statement]. In the following section, we develop our theoretical framework."
```

## Hypothesis Development Templates

### Single-Path Hypothesis
```
"[Theory] posits that [theoretical proposition]. In line with this reasoning, [empirical support] (Author, Year). 
Therefore, when [condition], [X] should [influence] [Y]. Accordingly:

Hypothesis 1: [X] is positively/negatively related to [Y]."
```

### Mediation Hypothesis
```
"We argue that [X] influences [Y] through [M]. Specifically, [X] first affects [M] because [mechanism1 - theory-grounded]. 
In turn, [M] shapes [Y] because [mechanism2]. Thus, [M] mediates the relationship between [X] and [Y].

Hypothesis 2: [M] mediates the positive/negative relationship between [X] and [Y]."
```

### Moderation Hypothesis
```
"We further propose that the effect of [X] on [Y] is contingent on [moderator W]. Specifically, when [W is high/present], 
[the mechanism connecting X to Y is strengthened/weakened] because [theoretical reason]. 
In contrast, when [W is low/absent], [the effect should be attenuated/enhanced].

Hypothesis 3: [W] moderates the relationship between [X] and [Y], such that the relationship is [stronger/weaker] 
when [W is high] than when [W is low]."
```

## Useful Academic Phrases Bank

### Acknowledging Prior Work
- "Prior research has established..."
- "Scholars have long recognized..."
- "A substantial body of work suggests..."
- "Recent advances in [field] have..."
- "Seminal work by [Author (Year)] demonstrates..."

### Signaling a Gap
- "Despite these advances..."
- "Notably, however..."
- "An important, yet understudied, question is..."
- "Surprisingly little attention has been paid to..."
- "Prior work has been limited in its ability to..."

### Connecting Theory to Hypothesis
- "Consistent with [Theory]..."
- "In line with this perspective..."
- "Drawing on [Theory], we expect..."
- "This argument is supported by..."
- "Theoretical and empirical work converges on..."

### Hedging Language (for uncertain claims)
- "may", "might", "could", "is likely to", "tends to"
- "we suggest", "we argue", "we propose", "we expect"
- "evidence indicates", "research suggests", "findings imply"
